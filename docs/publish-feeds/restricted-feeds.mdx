A **Restricted** feed is a subscriber-only feed. You still publish each signal once and the gateway still delivers it, but only to devices you approved. A subscriber proves which wallet they control by signing one message; your server decides whether that wallet may read; the gateway admits only the devices you granted. You can revoke a device, or every device of a wallet, when someone is no longer eligible.

Restricted is an **access policy** of an ordinary feed (`gateway_feed`), not a new connection mode. Everything on [Publish your first feed](/docs/publish-your-first-feed) still applies; this page adds who may read.

## Who decides what \{#roles}

| Role | Controls |
| --- | --- |
| **You**, the publisher | Which wallets and devices may read: your eligibility rule, approvals, rejections, revocations, invitations. Any billing, entirely in your own system |
| **The SAC gateway** | Enforces your grants on every read, stream and push. Holds your feed's registered policy and authentication origin, which the SAC team sets at your request |
| **The subscriber** | Which wallet they prove, and whether to keep the feed |

You cannot make your own feed Public by yourself, and the SAC team cannot approve a subscriber: the registration decides *whether* a feed is gated, and your decisions decide *who* gets through.

## The authorization flow \{#authorization-flow}

```text
subscriber adds link ─► phone asks YOUR server for a challenge
                    ─► wallet signs one message + device key signs the same bytes ─► request is pending
YOUR rule or admin  ─► approve ─► single-use invitation for that one device
phone redeems it    ─► YOUR server grants access at the GATEWAY ─► phone gets a session
phone reads feed    ─► GATEWAY checks the grant on every read, stream ticket and push target
YOUR server renews the grant while the device stays eligible, or revokes it
```

1. **Challenge.** The subscriber's phone asks your authentication endpoint for a challenge, naming the feed, the wallet and a device key it created for this feed.
2. **Wallet proof.** The wallet signs the challenge message once. It is plain text, says it is not a transaction, and moves no funds. The device key signs the same bytes, binding the request to that installation.
3. **Decision.** Your eligibility rule answers at once, or leaves the request pending for a person.
4. **Invitation.** On approval, your server issues a single-use invitation bound to that device key.
5. **Redemption.** The phone redeems the invitation with its device key. Your server grants access at the gateway and hands the phone a session.
6. **Reading.** The gateway admits the session on every read until the grant ends.
7. **Renewal or revocation.** Your server renews the grant while the device stays eligible, and revokes it when not.

The exact calls and fields are on [Restricted access reference](/docs/restricted-access-reference).

## Before you start \{#before-you-start}

1. **Decide your eligibility rule.** A person approves each request by hand, or your own system answers automatically, for example from membership records. See [Paid membership](/docs/recipe-paid-membership).
2. **Pick your authentication origin.** A public HTTPS origin, such as `https://auth.example.com`, where phones reach your server's `/access/v1` endpoint. No path, query or fragment. Plain HTTP works only on loopback, for local development.
3. **Get registered as Restricted at that origin** ([Connect to the SAC gateway](/docs/connect-to-gateway)). Until the registration is right, a correctly built server publishes nothing, by design.

## Step 1: Serve the authentication endpoint \{#serve-access-endpoint}

Phones call `/access/v1` at your authentication origin. It holds no credential: every call is checked by signature, and it tells a caller nothing about anyone else. Choose how you provide it.

<Tabs groupId="restricted-path">
<TabItem value="library" label="Publisher library (Go)" default>

The source repository's Go publisher library implements the whole publisher side in its `access` package: challenges, wallet and device-key verification, requests, invitations, redemption, grant sync with the gateway, renewal, and the admin API. The demo feed server `examples/demo-signals` (CopyTrading) is built on it and runs as a Restricted feed; start from it.

Beside the ordinary `PUBLISHER_*` settings, it reads:

| Variable | Default | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `PUBLISHER_AUTH_ORIGIN` | Required | The origin phones reach `/access/v1` at, exactly as registered. Missing or malformed refuses to start |
| `PUBLISHER_AUTH_ADDRESS` | Empty | A separate `host:port` listener for `/access/v1`. Empty serves it beside the token-protected `/v1` API |
| `PUBLISHER_ACCESS_GRANT_HOURS` | `6` (1 to 720) | How long a device's gateway grant runs before your server renews it |
| `PUBLISHER_ACCESS_INVITATION_MINUTES` | `5` (1 to 60) | How long an approved device has to redeem its invitation |
| `PUBLISHER_ACCESS_CHALLENGES_PER_HOUR` | `60` | Wallet challenges one client IP address may ask for per hour |

Publish `/access/v1` at your authentication origin through your HTTPS proxy or platform ingress. The rest of the API, including your admin routes under `/v1/access/…`, stays behind `PUBLISHER_API_TOKEN`.

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="custom" label="Your own backend">

Implement the same contract yourself:

1. **Serve `/access/v1`** at your authentication origin: `POST /access/v1/challenges`, `POST /access/v1/requests`, `POST /access/v1/requests/{id}/status` and `POST /access/v1/redeem`. Build the challenge message exactly as specified: the phone rebuilds it from the fields and refuses to sign anything else.
2. **Verify both signatures**: the wallet's Ed25519 signature and the device key's P-256 signature, over the same bytes, within the allowed clock skew.
3. **Store requests and decisions** in your own database, keyed by wallet and device.
4. **Grant and revoke at the gateway** with `GrantAccess`, `RevokeAccess` and `DescribeAccess` on the publisher API, authenticated with your publisher credential. Retry until the gateway confirms.
5. **State the policy in your manifest**: `"access": {"policy": "FEED_ACCESS_POLICY_RESTRICTED", "authOrigin": "https://auth.example.com"}` in its `feed` reference.

Every call, field and status is on [Restricted access reference](/docs/restricted-access-reference).

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

**Expected result:** your server starts and logs `access=restricted` with your origin (publisher library), or answers a challenge request at `https://<your origin>/access/v1/challenges` (your own backend).

## Step 2: Confirm the registration before publishing \{#fails-closed}

Before it publishes, the publisher library asks the gateway how it enforces your feed (`DescribeAccess`), and publishes only when the answer is **Restricted, at this origin**. Do the same in your own backend.

| Situation | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| The feed is not registered as Restricted, or at another origin | Nothing is published; the library refuses with `access_unconfirmed` and signals wait in your database. Ask the SAC team to check the registration |
| The gateway is unreachable | Same: `access_unconfirmed`, signals wait |
| A manifest claims a policy or origin the registration does not have | The gateway refuses it with `ACCESS_MISMATCH` |
| The registration is switched back to Public | The library stops publishing within about a minute |

`access_unconfirmed` is a delayed publication, never an audience nobody approved. When the registration is right, the waiting signals go out.

Origins are compared exactly after normalising (lowercase host, no default port, no trailing slash): `https://auth.example.com` and `https://auth.example.com:8443` are different origins.

## Step 3: Share the feed link \{#share-the-link}

```text
seekervault://feed?v=1&gateway=https%3A%2F%2Fgateway.example.com&server=3f1b2c4d-5e6f-4a7b-8c9d-0e1f2a3b4c5d&access=restricted
```

Share it anywhere. **A link alone grants no access.** The phone reads the policy and your authentication origin from the manifest the gateway serves, stamped from your registration; that is the only address a phone ever sends a wallet proof to. `access=restricted` is a floor: if the manifest then says Public, the phone refuses the feed. What the subscriber sees is on [Join a Restricted feed](/docs/join-restricted-feed).

## Step 4: Decide who gets in \{#decide}

Each request reaches your eligibility rule:

| Rule | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Manual approval** (the demo's default) | Every request waits for a person to approve or reject it |
| **Your own rule**, for example a membership lookup | Eligible wallets are approved at once; ineligible ones are rejected; anything else waits for a person |

Approving one device never approves another, even of the same wallet: a second phone is a second request. How to approve, reject, reissue, revoke and follow renewal is on [Manage subscriber access](/docs/manage-subscriber-access).

## Step 5: Verify end to end \{#verify}

1. Add the link on a phone with a wallet connected, and sign the access message.
2. The request appears as pending on your side.
3. Approve it. On its next check, every few seconds while SAC is open, the phone connects and the feed's signals appear.
4. Revoke it. After the gateway confirms, the phone shows that its access was revoked and receives nothing new.

## What the gateway enforces \{#gateway-enforcement}

| On a Restricted channel | Requires |
| --- | --- |
| The manifest | Nothing: a phone needs it to know where to prove a wallet |
| Listing and reading signals | A live grant's session, on every page |
| Live updates | A stream ticket issued only for a live grant, lasting no longer than it |
| Online/offline status | A live grant's session |
| Push | A per-device target registered under the grant. Restricted channels never use a shared push topic |

There is no anonymous fallback. When a device is revoked, the gateway stops admitting it on its next read and stream. **Revocation does not depend on push**, and **revocation cannot erase what was delivered**: signals already on a subscriber's phone stay there.

## Who knows what \{#who-knows-what}

| | Holds | Never holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your server | The wallet address that proved itself, the device key it bound, the phone's label, your decisions, invitations, grant IDs and a digest of each session | What a subscriber did with a signal: no amount, decision, transaction or result |
| The gateway | A grant ID, one opaque reference per wallet and one per device, the SHA-256 of the session, an expiry, and a push target while one is registered | The wallet address, the wallet signature, the device's label, or the session itself |
| The phone | A per-feed device key in the Android Keystore, the session, and its own record of the request | — |

Every approved subscriber receives the same publication: a Restricted feed gates who reads, it does not personalise what they read, and nothing is traded automatically.
